Notre Héritage

A House Built
on Patience

In 1927, perfumer Émile Lumière opened a modest atelier on a hillside in Grasse, then the undisputed capital of the world's flower fields. He had a single conviction: that a perfume should never smell like chemistry — it should smell like a place, a moment, a person.

Three generations later, that conviction has not changed. We still harvest with the same families of growers, still distil in copper alembics older than the house itself, and still refuse to release a composition until it has rested for a minimum of six weeks. Time, we believe, is the rarest ingredient of all.

"Perfume is the most intense form of memory. We are merely its editors."

— Émile Lumière, founder, 1927

L'Art de la Composition

The Making of a Maison Fragrance

From field to flacon, every Maison Lumière eau de parfum passes through four unhurried stages. None of them can be rushed, and none of them are outsourced.

I

The Harvest

Jasmine is picked at dawn before the sun touches the petals; rose centifolia is gathered in the cool of May mornings. Our grower partners have worked with the house for four generations — no intermediaries, no compromise.

II

The Distillation

Enfleurage and copper-alembic distillation extract absolutes with the delicacy industrial methods destroy. A single kilo of jasmine absolute requires eight hundred kilos of blossoms.

III

The Composition

Our perfumers blend by hand on marble, adjusting accords drop by drop until the formula sings — often over dozens of trials. Only one in four trials ever earns the Lumière name.

IV

The Maceration

The finished concentrate rests in dark glass for six weeks, allowing the materials to marry. Then it is hand-bottled in crystal, sealed with a gold collar, and numbered.
